Problem 3: Complex Roots and De Moivre's Theorem Live Core

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply De Moivre's theorem to find all $n$ distinct roots of a polynomial complex equation.
  • Use for loops and array pre-allocation to iteratively generate complex roots.
  • Visualize complex root distributions on an Argand diagram.
  • Verify the cyclic symmetry and zero-sum property of roots of unity ($\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} z_k = 0$).

Description

Finding all roots of complex polynomial equations is critical for stability analysis in control systems and structural vibration modes. In this exercise, you will calculate all five roots of $z^5 = 32$, plot their geometric distribution on the Argand plane, and verify their sum.

Required Grader Variables

Variable Name Expected Type Dimension Description
w double $1 \times 1$ scalar Target number $w = 32$
r_w double $1 \times 1$ scalar Modulus $|w| = 32$ via abs(w)
theta_w double $1 \times 1$ scalar Phase angle of $w$ in radians via angle(w)
z_roots double (complex) $1 \times 5$ row Vector containing all 5 complex roots
sum_roots double (complex) $1 \times 1$ scalar Sum of all 5 roots via sum(z_roots) ($\approx 0$)
Prediction Prompt: How many distinct roots does $z^5 = 32$ have in the complex plane? (By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, exactly 5). If they are distributed symmetrically around a circle of radius $32^{1/5} = 2$, what will their vector sum be? (Zero!).

Problem Tasks

Complete the following tasks in prob_6_3.m. By De Moivre's theorem, the $n$ roots of $w = re^{i\theta}$ are: $$z_k = r^{1/n}\exp\left(i\frac{\theta + 2\pi k}{n}\right), \quad k = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$$

Part A — Define the Target Number
Set w = 32. Calculate its magnitude r_w = abs(w) and angle theta_w = angle(w).

Part B — Calculate the 5 Roots
Pre-allocate z_roots = zeros(1, 5) and use a for loop (k = 0:4) to calculate all 5 roots of $z^5 = 32$. Store into array index k+1.

Part C — Visualise on an Argand Diagram
Plot the roots as points on the complex plane using plot(real(z_roots), imag(z_roots), 'ro') with grid on, axis equal, and axis labels.

Part D — Sum of the Roots
Calculate sum_roots = sum(z_roots), and confirm that it is zero within floating-point tolerance.

Staged Hints

Hint 1: Concept Hint (Roots of Complex Numbers)

The $n$ roots of $w = r e^{i\theta}$ all lie on a circle centered at the origin with radius $R = r^{1/n}$. They are spaced at equal angular intervals of $\Delta\theta = \frac{2\pi}{n}$ radians ($72^\circ$ for $n=5$), forming the vertices of a regular polygon.

Hint 2: MATLAB Syntax Hint

Pre-allocate z_roots = zeros(1, 5);. In MATLAB, array indices must be $\ge 1$, so for loop index k = 0:4, store the computed root into z_roots(k+1).

Hint 3: Code Skeleton 
% Part A
w = 32;
r_w = abs(w);
theta_w = angle(w);

% Part B
z_roots = zeros(1, 5);
for k = 0:4
    z_roots(k+1) = r_w^(1/5) * exp(1i * (theta_w + 2*pi*k)/5);
end

% Part C
% plot(real(z_roots), imag(z_roots), 'ro'); grid on; axis equal;

% Part D
sum_roots = sum(z_roots);

Self-Check & Expected Properties

  • r_w is 32 and theta_w is 0 (since 32 is a positive real number).
  • The first root ($k=0$) is $2e^{i(0)} = 2.0 + 0i$.
  • The magnitude of every root in z_roots must equal $2.0$ (verify with abs(z_roots)).
  • abs(sum_roots) must be less than $10^{-14}$.

Engineering Reflection

In digital filter design, what does the positioning of poles and zeros on the complex unit circle ($z$-plane) indicate about system stability? (Answer: Poles strictly inside the unit circle $|z| < 1$ indicate bounded-input bounded-output (BIBO) stability; poles outside cause unstable exponential oscillation.)
