Problem 5: Engineering Application - Work Done by a Force Optional Stretch

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply vector dot products to solve real-world engineering work and energy problems.
  • Resolve non-aligned force vectors into orthogonal components using trigonometry.
  • Perform mechanical work calculations for 3D crane rigging and lifting systems.
  • Interpret the physical meaning of positive, zero, and negative mechanical work.

Description

Mechanical work ($W$) is a scalar energy quantity defined by the dot product of a force vector and displacement vector ($W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{d}$). In this engineering application, you will evaluate energy transfer across 1D, 2D, and 3D mechanical loading scenarios.

Problem Tasks

Complete the following tasks in prob_2_5.m. Recall that mechanical work is defined as $W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{d}$.

Part A — Force Parallel to Displacement
A worker pushes a crate with a constant horizontal force of 50 N, moving it 10 m in the same direction.

  1. Create force vector F_push = [50, 0].
  2. Create displacement vector d_crate = [10, 0].
  3. Calculate work_A = dot(F_push, d_crate).

Part B — Force at an Angle to Displacement
A person pulls a suitcase with a force of 80 N at $30^\circ$ above horizontal, moving it 15 m horizontally.

  1. Convert the angle to radians: theta_rad = deg2rad(30).
  2. Resolve the force into components: $F_x = |\mathbf{F}|\cos\theta$, $F_y = |\mathbf{F}|\sin\theta$.
  3. Build F_suitcase = [Fx, Fy] and d_suitcase = [15, 0].
  4. Calculate work_B = dot(F_suitcase, d_suitcase).

Part C — 3D Case: Crane Lifting a Load
A construction crane exerts cable tension F_crane = [200, 150, 800] N to displace a structural pre-cast load by d_load = [5, -3, 10] m. Calculate work_C_3D = dot(F_crane, d_load).

Part D — Negative Work: Friction
A kinetic friction force of 25 N opposes an 8 m horizontal displacement.

  1. Create F_friction = [-25, 0].
  2. Create d_block = [8, 0].
  3. Calculate work_D_friction = dot(F_friction, d_block).

Staged Hints

Hint 1: Concept Hint (Work and Component Resolution)

Mechanical work is the scalar product of force and displacement: $W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{d} = \|\mathbf{F}\|\|\mathbf{d}\|\cos\theta$. When resolving force at angle $\theta$, the component doing work along the horizontal displacement is $F_x = \|\mathbf{F}\|\cos\theta$. The vertical component $F_y = \|\mathbf{F}\|\sin\theta$ does zero work because it is perpendicular to displacement!

Hint 2: MATLAB Syntax Hint

Use deg2rad(30) or 30 * pi / 180 to convert angles. Use dot(F, d) to compute the scalar work.

Hint 3: Code Skeleton 
% Part A
F_push = [50, 0];
d_crate = [10, 0];
work_A = dot(F_push, d_crate);

% Part B
theta_rad = deg2rad(30);
Fx = 80 * cos(theta_rad);
Fy = 80 * sin(theta_rad);
F_suitcase = [Fx, Fy];
d_suitcase = [15, 0];
work_B = dot(F_suitcase, d_suitcase);

% Part C
F_crane = [200, 150, 800];
d_load = [5, -3, 10];
work_C_3D = dot(F_crane, d_load);

% Part D
F_friction = [-25, 0];
d_block = [8, 0];
work_D_friction = dot(F_friction, d_block);

Self-Check & Expected Properties

  • work_A must be $50 \times 10 = 500$ Joules.
  • work_B is $80 \times \cos(30^\circ) \times 15 = 80 \times 0.8660 \times 15 \approx 1039.2$ Joules.
  • work_C_3D is $200(5) + 150(-3) + 800(10) = 1000 - 450 + 8000 = 8550$ Joules.
  • work_D_friction must be $-200$ Joules.

Engineering Reflection

In thermodynamic and energy conservation terms, why is the work done by friction negative ($-200\text{ J}$)? (Answer: Friction removes mechanical kinetic energy from the system, dissipating it into thermal internal energy (heat).)
