Background

Vector Representation in Engineering

In engineering and physical sciences, quantities such as force, velocity, acceleration, and displacement have both magnitude and direction, modeled in 3D Cartesian coordinates as: $$\mathbf{v} = v_x\mathbf{i} + v_y\mathbf{j} + v_z\mathbf{k}$$

MATLAB Vector Representation

In MATLAB, row vectors are enclosed in square brackets and separated by spaces or commas:

pos_2d = [3, 4];          % 2D row vector
vel_3d = [5, -2, 1];      % 3D row vector

Staged Hints

Hint 1: Concept Hint (Vector Arithmetic)

Vector addition and subtraction operate component-wise. If $\mathbf{u} = [u_1, u_2, u_3]$ and $\mathbf{v} = [v_1, v_2, v_3]$, then:

$$\mathbf{u} \pm \mathbf{v} = [u_1 \pm v_1, \; u_2 \pm v_2, \; u_3 \pm v_3]$$

Vectors must have identical dimensions. Scalar multiplication scales each component by the scalar factor $c$: $c\mathbf{u} = [c u_1, c u_2, c u_3]$.

Hint 2: MATLAB Syntax Hint

In MATLAB, row vectors are defined with commas or spaces: u = [1, 2, 3];. Standard operators (+, -, *) work directly on vectors of identical dimensions.

Engineering Reflection

If $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ represent two cable tension forces acting on a suspension bridge joint, what physical quantity does $\mathbf{w}_{\text{add}} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$ represent? (Answer: The resultant net force vector experienced by the joint.)
