Problem 4: Engineering Kinematics Independent Consolidation

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply first and second-order derivatives to model physical motion in 1D mechanics.
  • Derive velocity $v(t) = s'(t)$ and acceleration $a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t)$ from position-time equations.
  • Evaluate instantaneous dynamic quantities at a specified time using subs() and double().
  • Use the solve() equation solver to locate critical points (when a moving body comes to instantaneous rest).

Description

In mechanical and vehicle engineering, the calculus chain relating position, velocity, and acceleration is fundamental. In this problem, you will differentiate a position equation to calculate velocity and acceleration, and solve for the exact moments the particle comes to rest.

Prediction Prompt: Differentiate $s(t) = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t + 5$ by hand: $v(t) = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 = 6(t^2 - 3t + 2) = 6(t-1)(t-2)$. At what two times will the particle come to instantaneous rest? ($t = 1\text{ s}$ and $t = 2\text{ s}$).

Problem Tasks

Complete the following tasks in prob_4_4.m. Recall that $v(t) = s'(t)$ and $a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t)$.

Part A — Define the Position Function
A particle moves according to $s(t) = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t + 5$ ($t$ in seconds). Define symbolic variable t and store the function as s.

Part B — Velocity and Acceleration
Calculate the velocity function v (first derivative of s) and the acceleration function a (second derivative of s, or first derivative of v).

Part C — Evaluate at a Specific Time
Calculate the velocity v_at_4 and acceleration a_at_4 at $t = 4$ seconds, converting both to double.

Part D — When Is the Particle at Rest?
The particle is at rest when $v(t) = 0$. Use solve(v == 0, t) to find all values of t for which this holds, storing the result in t_rest.

Staged Hints

Hint 1: Concept Hint (Kinematics Chain)

Velocity is the time rate of change of position: $v(t) = \frac{ds}{dt}$. Acceleration is the time rate of change of velocity: $a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}$. A particle is momentarily at rest whenever its instantaneous velocity is zero ($v(t) = 0$).

Hint 2: MATLAB Syntax Hint

Declare syms t. Differentiate using diff(s, t) and diff(v, t). Solve for roots using solve(v == 0, t). Substitute using double(subs(v, t, 4)).

Hint 3: Code Skeleton 
syms t

% Part A
s = 2*t^3 - 9*t^2 + 12*t + 5;

% Part B
v = diff(s, t);
a = diff(v, t);

% Part C
v_at_4 = double(subs(v, t, 4));
a_at_4 = double(subs(a, t, 4));

% Part D
t_rest = solve(v == 0, t);

Self-Check & Expected Properties

  • v must be $6t^2 - 18t + 12$.
  • a must be $12t - 18$.
  • v_at_4 is $6(16) - 18(4) + 12 = 96 - 72 + 12 = 36$ m/s.
  • a_at_4 is $12(4) - 18 = 30$ $\text{m/s}^2$.
  • double(t_rest) should be [1; 2] seconds.

Engineering Reflection

If a piston in an internal combustion engine has $v(t) = 0$ at top-dead-center (TDC), why is the acceleration $a(t)$ at its maximum at that exact same instant? (Answer: The velocity is reversing direction, requiring the maximum net inertial force from Newton's second law $F = ma$.)
